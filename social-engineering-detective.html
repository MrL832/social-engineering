Exam-friendly definitions plus prevention methods.

Social Engineering

Definition: the manipulation of people to obtain confidential information or access.

Key idea: attackers exploit trust, fear, urgency, or helpfulness instead of hacking code.

  • Slow down and verify unusual requests.
  • Use official channels and call-backs.
  • Use MFA so a password alone isn’t enough.
Phishing

What it is: fake messages (email/text/DM) that try to trick you into clicking links, downloading files, or typing logins.

  • Check sender and the real domain.
  • Don’t log in via message links—type the official address yourself.
  • Report suspicious messages.
Blagging (Pretexting)

What it is: someone invents a believable story (a pretext) to persuade you to reveal information or grant access.

  • Verify identity (call-back to a trusted number).
  • Never share passwords or one-time codes.
  • Share the minimum information needed.
Shoulder Surfing

What it is: someone watches your screen/keyboard to steal information (PINs, passwords, private messages).

  • Cover keypads/screens and angle away from others.
  • Use privacy screen filters; avoid logging in in crowds.
  • Lock devices when you step away.
